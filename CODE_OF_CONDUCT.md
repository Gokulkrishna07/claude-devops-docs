# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Critique documentation and code, not people.

## Specifics

**Expected:** constructive feedback, patience with people learning, respect for differing experience levels and tooling choices, graceful acceptance of a "no" on a pull request.

**Not acceptable:** harassment of any kind, personal attacks, dismissiveness toward people newer than you, publishing others' private information.

## Contributions with real infrastructure in them

Failure-mode contributions come from real incidents, which means they can carry real detail. Before opening a pull request, strip your employer's service names, hostnames, account identifiers, and internal URLs. Use `<ANGLE_BRACKET>` placeholders. A maintainer will ask you to do this if you forget, but it is much better not to have pushed it in the first place — git history is forever.

## Enforcement

Report problems by opening a private security advisory or contacting a maintainer directly. Maintainers may edit, remove, or reject contributions and comments that don't fit these expectations, and will explain why when they do.
